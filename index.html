§ 06 — Recap

ICQSR 2026 Summary

This year, the QSR Section organized the 4th INFORMS Conference on Quality, Statistics, and Reliability (ICQSR), hosted by City University of Hong Kong, July 5–9, 2026. The conference theme was AI for Quality & Reliability.

Dates
July 5–9, 2026
Location
Hong Kong SAR, China
Host
City University of Hong Kong

The program opened on Sunday, July 5 with a Data Challenge workshop and lab tours, followed by four days of plenary talks, a distinguished lecture, a Leaders Forum, the Best Paper Competition, the Future Faculty Colloquium, and parallel technical sessions. Journal-featured sessions included the INFORMS Journal on Data Science, ENBIS, Technometrics, and IISE Transactions. Participants also joined a banquet aboard the Harbour Cruise Bauhinia, a networking reception on campus, and tours of CityU’s College of Computing and Embodied AI labs.

Other QSR Activities

QSR Webinars

The QSR Webinar Series brings research talks by QSR members to the wider community. Recordings of past webinars are available on the QSR YouTube channel.

Recent Webinar
Jump GP Surrogates for Non-stationary Systems Modeling
Prof. Chiwoo Park, Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering, University of Washington
Dr. Park introduced Jump Gaussian Process (Jump GP) surrogates, a class of models for non-stationary systems whose responses are smooth within regions but can change abruptly across the design space. The talk covered the original Jump GP framework, its extension to deep Gaussian processes for high-dimensional problems, and active learning strategies for efficient sequential data collection, with applications in smart manufacturing and autonomous materials design.
Dr. Park joined the University of Washington in 2024 from Florida State University. He received his B.S. from Seoul National University and his Ph.D. from Texas A&M University, and his research spans surrogate modeling, Bayesian sequential experimental design, shape data analysis, and digital twins for cyber-physical systems.
Fri, September 4, 2026 · 2:00–3:00 PM ET [Recording link — pending]
Upcoming Webinar
Robust Inference of Unseen Heterogeneous Physical Properties from Imperfect Data: A Unified Probabilistic Physics-Informed Machine Learning Framework
Prof. Jaesung Lee, Texas A&M University
Many biomedical and engineering systems are governed by hidden, spatially varying physical properties that can only be observed through noisy, low-resolution measurements. This talk presents recent advances in physics-informed machine learning for such statistical inverse problems, focusing on a unified probabilistic formulation that addresses ill-posedness, measurement noise, limited spatial resolution, and the balance between data and physics.
Dr. Lee is an Assistant Professor in the Wm Michael Barnes ’64 Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering at Texas A&M University. He received his Ph.D. in Industrial and Systems Engineering and M.S. in Statistics from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and was a finalist in the 2025 INFORMS QSR Best Refereed Paper Competition.
Wed, October 21, 2026 · 1:00–2:00 PM ET Register on Zoom →

INFORMS Student Chapter Funding

QSR supports INFORMS Student Chapters that organize events and initiatives highlighting quality, statistics, and reliability. Under the Fall 2026 call, one or more chapters are selected for funding of up to $300 per chapter. Chapters organize their events in Fall 2026 and are reimbursed after submitting an activity report. Priority goes to proposals that promote sustained engagement with the QSR Section rather than a one-time event.

Proposals were due July 31, 2026, and successful chapters were notified by August 17. Questions can be directed to Dr. Minhee Kim (mkim3@ufl.edu) or Dr. Ahmed Aziz Ezzat (aa2085@rutgers.edu).

IISE Transactions

Special Series · Rolling Submissions
Practice-Driven Research Challenges (PDRC)
Editors: Judy Jin (University of Michigan), Zhenyu (James) Kong (Virginia Tech), Kaibo Liu (University of Wisconsin–Madison), Jennifer Ryan (Lehigh University), Siqian Shen (University of Michigan)
A new series that strengthens the connection between industrial practice and academic research by spotlighting real-world, data-rich, and analytically complex problems. Two types of short papers are welcome, both with expedited review: Problem Statement papers (up to 10 pages) that describe a practical challenge and provide relevant datasets, and Solution papers (up to 15 pages) that address a problem posed in a Problem Statement paper. Full-length regular papers addressing these problems are also welcome.
